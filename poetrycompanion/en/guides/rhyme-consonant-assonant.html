Consonant and assonant rhyme: what they are and how to tell them apart

Rhyme creates sound patterns between lines. To identify a rhyme, compare the final words from the last stressed vowel onward. The rhyme is consonant or assonant depending on which sounds match.

What is consonant rhyme?

In consonant rhyme, all sounds—vowels and consonants—match from the final stressed vowel onward. For example, the Spanish words canción and atención share the same ending sound, «-ción».

What is assonant rhyme?

In assonant rhyme, only the vowels match from the final stressed vowel onward; consonants may differ. For example, the Spanish words casa and rama share the vowel pattern «a-a», while their consonants differ.

How to check a rhyme

  1. Find the last word in each line.
  2. Locate its stressed syllable and compare sounds from that vowel onward.
  3. If every sound matches, the rhyme is consonant; if only the vowels match, it is assonant.
  4. Read the lines together to make sure the repetition sounds natural in the poem.
Examples: canción / atención: consonant rhyme. Casa / rama: assonant rhyme.

Choose the rhyme that serves the poem

Consonant rhyme makes a closer sound match; assonant rhyme leaves more room for variation. Neither is better by itself. Try alternatives, read them in the stanza, and keep the one that supports the meaning and rhythm you want.
